# Step 7: Estimate ("What it costs to build.")

**What it's for:** turning the saved design and the plan into a cost range, then exporting plans for the field.

**What's on screen**
- **Total build estimate:** earthworks, layout and design, with contingency, shown as a low to high range.
- **Line items:** quantities computed from the design geometry, each with a cost range.
- **Planning allowances:** interventions from your plan that the design tools don't compute (tanks, plantings, fuel reduction and so on), at the quantity set on the Ideas step times a deliberately wide starter rate.
- **Design fee** (for computed keyline and swale layouts) and **Field time (planning)** lines.
- **Contingency:** a percentage of the build (15% by default), editable.
- **Edit unit costs:** change the low and high rate for any cost item to match local prices; a reset returns to the default.
- **Export plans** opens Export: a PDF plan set (11 × 17 sheets), GIS files (georeferenced layers in longitude and latitude), a DXF CAD drawing and GPS tractor paths. A link to the autonomous keyline robot is shown as not available yet.

**How to read it**
- Only saved design elements and plan items count. Assistant proposals that haven't been accepted are not in the estimate.
- Planning allowances are order-of-magnitude numbers covering DIY at the low end and contractor installation at the high end.
- Costs are ranges because unit costs vary by region, soil and contractor. Default rates are starting points; replace them with local quotes.
- Planning-grade designs are marked draft here and on every export.

**Common problems**
- An empty estimate: add interventions to your plan on the Ideas step, or lay out keylines or swales on the Design step.

**When to move on:** export the plans you need.
