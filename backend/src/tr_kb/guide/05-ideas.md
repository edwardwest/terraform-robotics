# Step 5: Ideas ("Interventions that fit this land.")

**What it's for:** choosing which interventions to plan, from a catalog covering water harvesting, earthworks, soil, plants and trees, fire, animals, and structures and access.

**What's on screen**
- Category chips filter the list: All, Water, Earthworks, Soil, Plants and trees, Fire, Animals, Structures and access.
- Ideas are grouped as **Good fit**, **Worth a look** and **Not suited here** (collapsed). Each card shows why it fits (which goals it serves, how it suits the slope), concerns (budget, upkeep, area, a missing creek or building), notes on what isn't known yet, permit and safety cautions, upkeep, a cost tier ($ to $$$$), NRCS practice codes, and further reading.
- **Add to plan** puts an idea in the plan; **In plan** shows it's there, and clicking it again removes it. For ideas with a planning allowance, a quantity field (sq ft or acres, feet, gallons or a count) sets how much goes into the estimate.
- Keyline patterns and swales show **Lay out on the Design step**, because the Design step computes them from the terrain model.
- **Your plan** counts what you've chosen; **Continue to design** opens the plan in build order.

**How the ranking works**
- It is plain code, not the assistant: goal match weighted by your goal order, then penalties for budget, upkeep and constraints you set. Site area rules out ideas that need more land; slope and valleys are checked once the terrain model exists.
- Not suited means a hard requirement is missing (too little land, no creek, no valley with a keypoint). Worth a look means it fits but has a concern, or doesn't serve your chosen goals.

**Common problems**
- Everything is Worth a look: no goals set yet. Go to the Goals step.
- Slope notes say the terrain model is needed: build it on the Survey step.

**When to move on:** once your plan has what you want to do first, choose Continue to design.
