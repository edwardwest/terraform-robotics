# Step 2: Goals ("What do you want from this land?")

**What it's for:** recording what the person wants from the land, so the Ideas step can rank interventions that fit instead of assuming one answer.

**What's on screen**
- **Goals:** ten goal chips (Store and save water, Stop erosion, Reduce wildfire risk, Grow food, Build soil health, Habitat and biodiversity, Raise animals, Farm income, Shade wind and comfort, Manage stormwater). Pick up to five; the order you pick them in is their priority, shown as numbers. Click a chosen goal again to remove it.
- **The land:** **How the land is used** (town lot, home on acreage, small farm, ranch, orchard or vineyard, woodland), **Buildings on site** (yes or no), **Water sources** (rain only, well, municipal, irrigation district, creek or spring) and **Annual rainfall (in)** if known.
- **Budget and effort:** **Budget**, **Timeline** and **Upkeep you want** (low, medium, high).
- **Constraints:** renting, HOA rules, limited machinery access, sensitive habitat on site, strict permitting.
- **Your vision:** a few sentences in your own words.
- **Save goals** saves the brief. **Talk it through with the assistant** opens the assistant and starts a short interview; its suggested answers appear in the assistant panel with **Apply to goals**, which fills the form and saves.
- **Continue to survey** goes on to elevation data; **See ideas now** jumps to the Ideas step without terrain.

**Decisions here**
- Which goals matter most. The top goal weighs most in the ranking, so put first what you'd fund first.
- Upkeep and budget: these push high-maintenance or expensive ideas down the list rather than hiding them.

**Common problems**
- Ideas look generic: no goals are chosen yet. Choose at least one goal and save.

**When to move on:** once the goals and constraints read true, choose Continue to survey (or See ideas now).
